# Frontend-Practicum

## Juniper restaurant

A single-page restaurant concept built with plain HTML5 and CSS3. No JavaScript, frameworks, build step, or dependencies are required. Open `index.html` in a browser.

## Assessment checklist

- Semantic header, navigation, main, sections, and footer; one h1.
- Navigation anchors reach the menu, story, location, and reservation sections.
- Three menu categories: starters, mains, and desserts.
- Global border-box sizing and custom properties for the color palette.
- Flexbox: navigation, buttons, dish names/prices, reservation layout.
- Grid: three-column menu, about section, and footer.
- Responsive breakpoints at 1050px, 760px, and 380px; reduced-motion support, keyboard focus styles, skip link, and image descriptions.
- Reservations use telephone and email links, not an unconnected booking form.

## Replace before publishing

Juniper is fictional. The address, opening hours, menu, and reservation number are demonstration content. The phone uses a UK drama/example number and `juniper.example` is not a working email domain. Replace these with your own approved details. The map link opens the neighborhood, not a verified restaurant location.

Photography: Unsplash images by Jay Wennington (hero) and Nick Karvounis (dining room). Fonts: DM Sans and DM Serif Display from Google Fonts. These external resources need an internet connection; the font stacks include fallbacks.

## Your individual GitHub repository

Create your own empty GitHub repository. From this folder, run `git init`, then make real, small commits as you independently develop and understand your site. Add your repository as the remote and push your work. Do not fabricate a development history for the finished files.

Possible future commit units: personalize the restaurant identity, refine the menu, improve mobile spacing, add your own local photography, and verify accessibility. Commit each only after doing that work.

## Team and presentation

Agree on a restaurant identity and visual direction with your team of 4 to 5, but implement separately in your own repos. Meet to debug and discuss approaches without copying code. Follow your assessment's policy on AI assistance and disclose it where required.

Before presenting, be ready to explain the semantic landmarks, anchor links, the difference between Flexbox and Grid, custom properties, media queries, and why the reservation links do not constitute a booking backend. Test on phone, tablet, and desktop, and confirm `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` does not exceed the viewport width.
